What is story points in Agile?
	Story points in Agile are a unit of measure used to estimate the effort required to complete a user story or a task within a software development project. Rather than estimating in hours, which can be inaccurate and can lead to micromanagement, Agile teams use story points to gauge the relative complexity, risk, and effort involved in completing a particular piece of work.
	Story points are typically assigned using techniques like Planning Poker, where team members collectively assign points based on their understanding of the task's complexity. The Fibonacci sequence (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, etc.) is often used to assign story points, reflecting the notion that as the task's complexity increases, the level of uncertainty about the effort involved also increases.
	It's important to note that story points are a relative measure, not an absolute one. For example, a task assigned 5 story points might not necessarily take five times as long to complete as a task assigned 1 story point. Story points help Agile teams forecast their capacity for work in upcoming iterations or sprints and allow for more accurate planning and prioritization.

Story points in simple terms?
	Think of story points like measuring distances with different units. In regular life, you might measure short distances in meters or feet, medium distances in kilometers or miles, and long distances in hundreds of kilometers or miles. Similarly, in Agile, we use story points to measure the size or complexity of the tasks we need to do.

	But instead of using meters or miles, we use story points. These points help us understand how much work a task might take, but they're not about measuring time directly. They're more like a way for the team to agree on how big or small a task is compared to others.

	So, when we say something has 5 story points, it means it's about the same size or complexity as other tasks we've given 5 story points to in the past. It's a way for the team to communicate and plan together without worrying too much about exact time estimates.
